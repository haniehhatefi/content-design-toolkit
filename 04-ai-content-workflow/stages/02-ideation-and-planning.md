## 1. Purpose and scope

Stage 02 translates research and brief into a confirmed content approach. Its purpose is to explore possible directions, establish requirements, and produce a content brief that is specific enough to guide creation.

The stage answers three questions: What is the content trying to achieve? What approach and structure will serve the audience and context? What are the requirements and constraints that content creation must work within? When these are answered and confirmed, drafting has a clear foundation. When they are not, creation is guided by assumption rather than considered direction.

**The content brief as the central output**

The content brief produced at this stage defines the direction for all subsequent work. It carries objectives, audience, context, format, structure, voice and tone requirements, and applicable constraints through to content creation. A brief that is vague, incomplete, or not confirmed by the responsible practitioner creates downstream problems that are expensive to resolve at later stages.

The practitioner owns and approves the brief. AI can help generate, structure, and explore options as inputs to the brief; it cannot determine what the brief should say.

**Relationship with Stage 01**

Stage 02 builds on research findings from stage 01. Research findings inform the direction of ideation — they provide evidence about existing content, audience needs, terminology, and gaps. However, research findings do not automatically determine the content approach. The practitioner interprets the findings and decides what they mean for the work.

If significant research was not conducted at stage 01 — for example, because a brief was supplied externally — the practitioner should still confirm that the objectives, audience, and constraints are understood before ideation begins. Stage 02 should not proceed on assumptions that have not been verified.

## 2. When to use AI

**AI assistance is appropriate when:**

- Generating a range of possible content approaches or structures for the practitioner to evaluate
- Producing alternative formulations of requirements, objectives, or scope statements for review
- Drafting or structuring a content brief from supplied objectives and constraints, for the practitioner to review and confirm
- Exploring structural options for specific content types — for example, different ways to organise a set of related topics
- Generating initial outlines or content hierarchies based on a confirmed approach
- Identifying potential structural problems or inconsistencies in a proposed outline

**AI assistance is not appropriate when:**

- Selecting the content approach — AI can generate options; the practitioner chooses
- Determining what the audience needs or what the content should achieve — these are human judgements informed by research, not outputs AI can generate independently
- Approving or finalising the content brief — the brief must be confirmed by the responsible practitioner before stage 03 begins
- Deciding which research findings are relevant or what they imply for the content direction

**A note on AI-generated ideas**

AI can generate a large number of plausible-sounding content approaches quickly. Volume is not quality. The practitioner evaluates each option against the research findings, audience needs, and task requirements — not against how confidently or fluently the idea was expressed. An AI-generated approach that sounds reasonable but does not address the research findings should not be selected simply because it appears complete.

The goal of AI-assisted ideation is to surface a broader range of options for human evaluation, not to shortcut the evaluation itself.

## 3. Required human inputs

The following must be in place before AI-assisted activities begin.

**Reviewed research findings or supplied brief**

The practitioner must have reviewed the stage 01 research findings — or, where stage 01 was not conducted, an equivalent supplied brief — and understand what the available evidence establishes about the audience, existing content, gaps, and constraints. Ideation without a clear evidence base produces approaches that may not hold up in review.

**Defined objectives**

What is the content meant to achieve? The practitioner must be able to state the objective clearly. AI-generated content approaches will be evaluated against this objective; if it is not established first, evaluation has no stable basis.

**Audience understanding**

Who is the content for, and in what context will they encounter it? The practitioner must have a sufficient understanding of the target audience — their goals, context, knowledge level, and needs — to evaluate whether a proposed approach serves them.

**Applicable standards and constraints**

The practitioner identifies the applicable voice and tone guidelines, content standards, format constraints, length requirements, accessibility requirements, and any compliance or legal constraints. Their status should be clear: mandatory requirements must be met, while recommendations and contextual guidance should inform the evaluation of each approach. AI-generated options must not override applicable mandatory requirements.

**Criteria for a usable brief**

The practitioner should know what a complete, usable brief looks like for this task — what it must include, what level of specificity is needed for content creation to proceed, and who will use it. A brief produced for a solo task may be less formal than one that will pass to another contributor.

## 4. AI-assisted activities

**Generating content approach options**

Given a clear brief and research findings, AI can generate a range of possible approaches for the practitioner to evaluate. For each option, the practitioner considers: Does it address the objective? Is it appropriate for the audience? Does it fit the constraints? Options that do not meet these criteria are not carried forward.

*Illustrative example: A content designer developing guidance for a new user onboarding flow could ask AI to generate five structural approaches — tutorial, progressive disclosure, checklist, video-led, and human-led — with a brief rationale for each. The practitioner evaluates which is most appropriate for the audience's context and existing product conventions, selects one, and develops it into a brief.*

**Structuring and drafting a content brief**

AI can help draft or structure a content brief from objectives, audience description, and constraints supplied by the practitioner. The practitioner reviews the draft against the requirements, corrects inaccuracies or omissions, and confirms the brief before it passes to stage 03. AI assistance at this point reduces the administrative effort of brief-writing; it does not reduce the practitioner's responsibility for its content.

**Producing and refining content outlines**

Once an approach is selected, AI can generate initial outlines or content hierarchies. The practitioner reviews these against the brief — confirming that the structure serves the objective and audience, is consistent with the requirements, and does not introduce assumptions not established in the brief.

**Identifying structural issues**

AI can flag potential structural problems in a proposed outline: gaps in coverage, inconsistent level of detail, topics that may overlap, or sections that do not clearly relate to the stated objective. The practitioner evaluates whether the flagged issue is genuine and decides how to address it.

## 5. Human responsibilities

**Interpreting research findings**

Research findings inform ideation; they do not determine it. The practitioner interprets what the findings mean for the content approach — which gaps matter, which audience needs are most important to address, which existing content can be built on. This interpretation is a human judgement that cannot be derived from the research itself.

**Selecting the content approach**

The practitioner chooses the content approach. When AI has generated options, the practitioner evaluates each one against the objective, audience, evidence base, and constraints, and selects the approach that best serves the task. The practitioner should be able to explain why the chosen approach was selected — not because AI generated it, but because it addresses the requirements.

**Approving the content brief**

The practitioner reviews, corrects, and confirms the content brief before it is used to guide stage 03. A brief approved by the practitioner carries their authority and accountability. If significant elements are delegated to AI without review, that accountability is unclear.

The brief is confirmed when it is specific enough to guide content creation without relying on unstated assumptions, and when the practitioner is satisfied that it accurately represents the objectives, audience, requirements, and constraints. Any accepted assumptions or outstanding questions should be documented.

**Ensuring the brief is actionable**

A brief that is too vague will produce content that is impossible to evaluate at review. The practitioner confirms that the brief includes enough specificity — particularly around objectives, audience, voice, and format — that stage 03 can proceed without returning to stage 02 to resolve ambiguities.

## 6. Inputs and expected outputs

**Required inputs**

- Reviewed research findings from stage 01, or an equivalent supplied brief
- Defined task objectives and audience description
- Applicable voice, tone, and content standards
- Format, length, and structural constraints
- Accessibility, compliance, or other applicable requirements
- Criteria for a usable brief

**Expected outputs**

- Selected content approach, with a documented rationale
- Content structure or outline, confirmed by the practitioner
- Completed and confirmed content brief, including: objectives, audience, context, format, structure, voice and tone requirements, constraints, and applicable standards
- Notes on significant ideation decisions or alternatives considered, where these are relevant to stage 03

**Output format**

The content brief is the principal output of this stage. Use `../templates/content-brief-template.md` to structure it. The template ensures that the brief includes the information stage 03 requires. For tasks where the brief will be passed to another contributor, the template is the minimum; additional context may be needed.

## 7. Checkpoints and decision criteria

### Direction checkpoint

The Direction checkpoint applies at stage 02. It confirms that the objectives, audience, and constraints are established before AI-assisted ideation begins.

**What to confirm before starting:**

- The task objectives are defined and agreed
- The target audience is understood
- Applicable voice, tone, and content standards are identified
- Format, length, and structural constraints are confirmed
- Accessibility, compliance, or other applicable requirements are established
- The criteria for a complete, usable brief are established

The Direction checkpoint here is about ensuring ideation has a clear foundation. AI-generated content approaches will only be useful if the practitioner can evaluate them against established requirements. Without this foundation, evaluation becomes impressionistic rather than principled.

**Decision: Is the direction established?**

| Outcome | Action |
|---|---|
| Objectives, audience, and constraints are clear | Proceed to AI-assisted ideation |
| Objectives or audience understanding are insufficient | Establish these before ideation begins — return to stage 01 or clarify with stakeholders |
| Constraints are unclear | Identify and confirm constraints; proceeding without them risks producing approaches that cannot be used |

### Alignment checkpoint

An Alignment checkpoint applies after AI-assisted ideation activities, before the content approach and brief are confirmed.

**What to check:**

- Does the selected content approach address the stated objective and serve the target audience?
- Is the approach consistent with the research findings and evidence base?
- Does it meet the applicable voice, tone, and content standards?
- Is the content brief complete and specific, with assumptions and outstanding questions explicitly documented and any unresolved issues that could affect content creation addressed?
- Has the practitioner actively selected the approach — not simply accepted the first plausible option AI generated?

**Decision: Continue, redirect, or revise?**

| Outcome | Action |
|---|---|
| Approach and brief are confirmed | Proceed to stage 03 |
| Brief has gaps or unresolved ambiguities | Complete or clarify before proceeding |
| Selected approach does not adequately address the objective or evidence | Revisit the options or generate new ones; evaluate against requirements |
| Fundamental direction problem | Return to stage 01 to review research findings or clarify objectives |

## 8. Stage checklist

**Direction**
- [ ] Task objectives are defined and agreed
- [ ] Target audience is understood
- [ ] Applicable content standards are identified
- [ ] Format and structural constraints are confirmed
- [ ] Other applicable requirements (accessibility, compliance) are established
- [ ] Criteria for a usable brief are established

**AI-assisted activities**
- [ ] AI was given grounded context — objectives, audience, constraints — before generating options
- [ ] AI-generated approaches have been evaluated against requirements, not selected on appearance

**Human decisions**
- [ ] The content approach has been selected by the practitioner with a documented rationale
- [ ] The content brief has been reviewed, corrected where necessary, and confirmed by the practitioner
- [ ] The brief is specific enough to guide stage 03 without relying on unstated assumptions; accepted assumptions and outstanding questions are documented

**Output**
- [ ] Content brief is complete and uses the content brief template
- [ ] Content structure or outline is confirmed
- [ ] Significant ideation decisions are noted where relevant to stage 03

## 9. Handoff to next stage

Before moving to stage 03 (Content creation), confirm the following are in place and documented.

**What to pass forward**

- The confirmed content brief (using `../templates/content-brief-template.md`)
- The confirmed content structure or outline
- The rationale for the selected approach, particularly if alternatives were considered
- Notes on any decisions or constraints that stage 03 should be aware of
- Relevant voice, tone, and content standards, or references to where they are documented

**What to record**

For tasks where AI generated approaches or structures that required significant evaluation, note in `../templates/decision-and-iteration-log.md`:
- Which approaches were considered and why the selected approach was chosen
- Any AI-generated suggestions that were rejected and why

**Handoff template**

For collaborative tasks — where stage 03 will be carried out by a different contributor — use `../templates/handoff-template.md` to transfer the brief and relevant context. Include a summary of the ideation decisions and any constraints the person creating content needs to understand before beginning.
