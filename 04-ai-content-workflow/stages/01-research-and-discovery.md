## 1. Purpose and scope

Stage 01 establishes the foundation for the content work that follows. Its purpose is to understand what already exists, what the evidence says, and where gaps or opportunities lie — before any ideation or drafting begins.

Working from a clear research foundation reduces avoidable rework. Content developed without it is more likely to drift from audience needs, duplicate existing material, or rest on assumptions that review and testing later disprove.

The stage is relevant for a wide range of tasks: auditing existing content before a revision, synthesising user research before a new content area, reviewing competitor or comparative examples, or identifying terminology gaps before a localisation project. The research questions and scope will differ, but the underlying discipline — human-defined questions, AI-supported organisation, human evaluation of findings — applies consistently.

**What this stage is not**

This stage organises and synthesises evidence from supplied sources. It does not independently generate original research, conduct user testing, or validate factual claims by searching the internet. AI assistance at this stage is applied to material the practitioner has provided, not to material the AI retrieves or invents. If original user research is needed, that activity precedes or runs in parallel with this stage; its outputs then become inputs here.

## 2. When to use AI

**AI assistance is appropriate when:**

- Organising a large body of existing content, documentation, or research notes into a usable structure
- Identifying recurring themes, patterns, or terminology within supplied source materials
- Summarising lengthy documents or research findings to surface key points
- Flagging potential gaps in coverage or areas that may warrant further investigation, based on the supplied materials
- Generating initial audit frameworks or question sets from a brief — for the practitioner to review and refine

**AI assistance is not appropriate when:**

- Defining the research questions or audit objectives — these must be established by the practitioner
- Independently sourcing evidence — AI should not retrieve, generate, or present information as research when it was not part of the supplied materials
- Assessing the quality, credibility, or applicability of evidence — these judgements require the practitioner's domain knowledge and professional assessment
- Drawing conclusions or recommendations from research findings — AI can surface patterns; it cannot determine what the patterns mean for the content work

**A note on AI limitations at this stage**

AI assistants can produce confident-sounding summaries that are not fully grounded in the supplied source material. This is a particular risk when summarising large or complex document sets, or when the prompt does not establish clear grounding constraints. See `03-ai-content-creation/grounding-guide.md` for guidance on establishing grounding before AI-assisted research activities, and `03-ai-content-creation/failure-modes.md` for the specific failure modes most relevant to research synthesis tasks.

## 3. Required human inputs

The following must be established by the practitioner before AI-assisted activities begin.

**Research questions and objectives**

What is the research meant to find out? The practitioner defines the specific questions the stage should answer — for example: What content currently exists in this area? Where are the gaps? What terminology do users use? What do comparable examples look like? Vague objectives produce unfocused synthesis; specific questions produce usable findings.

**Source materials**

The practitioner identifies and supplies the materials AI will work with: existing content, documentation, research notes, user feedback, style guides, competitive examples, or other reference materials relevant to the task. AI-assisted synthesis applies only to what the practitioner has provided.

**Scope and constraints**

What is in scope and what is out? The practitioner establishes the boundaries: content type, product or service area, language, audience, and any constraints on what the research should consider. Scope constraints prevent synthesis from ranging beyond the task at hand.

**Evaluation criteria**

What will make the findings useful? The practitioner should have a clear sense of what a good research summary looks like for this task — what it needs to include, what level of detail is appropriate, and what format will serve stage 02.

## 4. AI-assisted activities

The following activities are appropriate for AI assistance at this stage, applied to materials the practitioner has supplied.

**Content auditing and organisation**

AI can help inventory and categorise existing content — grouping by topic, format, audience, or status. This is particularly useful when the volume of existing material makes manual review time-consuming.

*Illustrative example: A content designer reviewing 60 existing UX error messages before a redesign could ask AI to categorise them by error type, identify which follow a consistent pattern, and flag those that deviate — producing a structured starting point rather than a manual spreadsheet.*

**Theme and pattern identification**

AI can identify recurring themes, terminology, or structural patterns across supplied documents or research notes. This helps surface what the material emphasises and where coverage is uneven.

**Research summarisation**

AI can produce structured summaries of supplied research documents, drawing out key findings relevant to the defined research questions. The practitioner should specify the questions the summary should address, rather than asking for a general summary.

**Gap identification**

Based on the scope and supplied materials, AI can flag areas that appear underrepresented or absent. These are candidate gaps — they should be evaluated by the practitioner before being treated as confirmed findings.

**Audit framework drafting**

AI can generate a draft framework of review questions or criteria for a content audit, based on a supplied brief or objective. The practitioner reviews and refines this before using it.

## 5. Human responsibilities

The following cannot be delegated to AI at this stage.

**Defining the research questions**

The practitioner sets the agenda. AI organises and synthesises in response to that agenda; it does not determine what the research should find out.

**Selecting and providing source materials**

The practitioner decides which materials are relevant and provides them. AI-assisted synthesis is only as good as the sources it works from. Supplying incomplete, outdated, or unrepresentative material will produce misleading findings.

**Evaluating AI-generated summaries and findings**

The practitioner reads and assesses every AI-generated summary before treating it as a research finding. AI summarisation can omit important nuances, misrepresent source material, or present a confident synthesis that does not accurately reflect the sources. The practitioner checks the summary against the source materials.

**Assessing evidence quality**

Not all supplied materials are equally reliable, current, or applicable. The practitioner assesses whether each source is credible and relevant, and notes any limitations in the evidence base. AI cannot make this assessment.

**Confirming or rejecting candidate gaps**

AI can flag potential gaps in coverage. The practitioner decides whether a flagged gap is a genuine finding — one that should inform stage 02 — or an artefact of the scope or the source materials provided.

**Deciding when research is sufficient**

The practitioner determines when enough has been understood to move to ideation and planning. Continuing to research without purpose delays the work without improving it.

## 6. Inputs and expected outputs

**Required inputs**

- Defined research questions or audit objectives
- Relevant existing content, documentation, or research materials
- Brief or task description, where available
- Relevant constraints: scope, audience, format, language, applicable standards
- Evaluation criteria for the research summary, including the required coverage, level of detail, and format

**Expected outputs**

- Organised findings relevant to the defined research questions
- Summary of relevant existing content, with notes on coverage and gaps
- Identified candidate gaps or opportunities, confirmed by the practitioner
- Notes on evidence quality and limitations, including any significant gaps in the source material
- A research summary ready to inform stage 02 ideation and planning

**Output format**

The research summary does not need to follow a fixed format. It should be legible and useful to the person who will work from it in stage 02 — whether that is the same practitioner or a different contributor. For collaborative tasks, structured notes with clear headings are preferable to informal annotations.

## 7. Checkpoints and decision criteria

### Direction checkpoint

The Direction checkpoint applies at stage 01. It must be established before AI-assisted activities begin.

**What to confirm before starting:**

- The research questions or audit objectives are defined and agreed
- The source materials are identified and available
- The scope and constraints are established
- The criteria for a useful research summary are established

The Direction checkpoint at stage 01 sets the conditions for the entire task. A poorly defined research agenda at this stage will produce findings that cannot reliably support ideation and planning in stage 02.

**Decision: Is the direction established?**

| Outcome | Action |
|---|---|
| Research questions and scope are clear | Proceed to AI-assisted activities |
| Research questions are unclear or incomplete | Complete the direction before beginning — a vague research agenda produces unfocused findings |
| Source materials are not yet available | Identify and obtain the materials before beginning synthesis work |

### Alignment checkpoint

An Alignment checkpoint applies after AI-assisted synthesis activities, before findings are treated as confirmed research output.

**What to check:**

- Does the AI-generated summary accurately reflect the supplied source materials?
- Has the AI introduced assumptions, generalisations, or conclusions not grounded in the materials?
- Do the candidate gaps reflect genuine absences in the source material, or are they artefacts of an incomplete source set?
- Are the findings relevant to the defined research questions?

**Decision: Continue, redirect, or revise?**

| Outcome | Action |
|---|---|
| Summary accurately reflects the sources and addresses the research questions | Confirm as research findings; proceed to stage exit |
| Summary has recoverable issues — specific inaccuracies or omissions | Correct at this stage — revise the summary or re-prompt with clearer constraints |
| Summary reflects a direction problem — research questions were too vague | Return to the Direction checkpoint; revise the research questions before continuing |

## 8. Stage checklist

Use this checklist to confirm that stage 01 is complete before moving to stage 02.

**Direction**
- [ ] Research questions or audit objectives are defined
- [ ] Source materials are identified and have been supplied
- [ ] Scope and constraints are established
- [ ] Evaluation criteria for the research output are established

**AI-assisted activities**
- [ ] AI assistance was grounded in supplied source materials
- [ ] AI-generated summaries have been reviewed against source materials by the practitioner

**Human evaluation**
- [ ] Every AI-generated finding or summary has been read and assessed by the practitioner
- [ ] Evidence quality and limitations have been noted
- [ ] Candidate gaps have been confirmed or rejected by the practitioner
- [ ] The practitioner has judged the findings sufficient to inform stage 02

**Research output**
- [ ] Organised findings are documented
- [ ] The research summary is legible and useful for the next stage
- [ ] Any significant limitations in the evidence base are noted

## 9. Handoff to next stage

Before moving to stage 02 (Ideation and planning), confirm the following are in place and documented.

**What to pass forward**

- Research findings, organised and confirmed by the practitioner
- Summary of existing content and identified gaps
- Notes on evidence quality and any limitations in the source base
- The original research questions, so that stage 02 can confirm the findings address them
- Any relevant source materials that stage 02 may need to reference

**What to record**

For tasks where the research involved significant AI-assisted synthesis, note in the Decision and iteration log:
- Which source materials were used
- What the AI was asked to do
- Any discrepancies found between AI-generated summaries and source materials, and how they were resolved

**Handoff template**

For collaborative tasks — where stage 02 will be carried out by a different contributor — use `../templates/handoff-template.md` to transfer the research summary and relevant context.
