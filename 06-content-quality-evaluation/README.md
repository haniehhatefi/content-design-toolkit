# Phase 7: Content Quality Evaluation

---

## Overview

Phase 7 provides a framework for evaluating content quality systematically — both within the content creation workflow and as a standalone activity applied to existing content. It extends the toolkit's existing evaluation tools without replacing them.

The output evaluation rubric in Phase 4 and the human oversight checkpoints in Phase 5 establish how quality is assessed during content creation. Phase 7 addresses three questions those phases do not fully answer:

- What quality dimensions apply beyond those a complete brief establishes?
- How should a quality evaluation be structured as a deliberate process, not only as a checkpoint step within a larger workflow?
- How should quality criteria be applied to existing content collections, not only to content currently in progress?

## Relationship to existing toolkit phases

**Phase 4 — Output evaluation rubric:** Provides five dimensions for assessing a single AI-assisted output against a brief at a checkpoint. Phase 7 extends the quality dimensions framework to include criteria not dependent on a brief, and addresses evaluation outside the creation workflow. The Phase 4 rubric remains the appropriate tool within that workflow.

**Phase 5 — Human oversight checkpoints:** Establishes the five human review stages and their responsibilities. Phase 7's evaluation methodology explains how quality findings connect to those checkpoints; it does not introduce new checkpoints or additional approval steps.

**Phase 6 — Practical experiments:** Applied the toolkit's evaluation methods to constructed scenarios and identified specific limits of brief-based compliance review. Phase 7 generalises those findings into reusable frameworks applicable across a wider range of content types and contexts.

---

## Three evaluation contexts

Quality dimensions in this phase are organised around three evaluation contexts. These are not mutually exclusive: a single dimension may require more than one context for a complete assessment.

**Brief-assessable** — Dimensions that can be evaluated using the brief and supplied reference materials: voice standards, grounding documents, accessible language guidance, and scope definitions. This aligns with the brief-based compliance review examined in Phase 6 Experiment 05.

**Product- or expertise-dependent** — Dimensions that require access to the actual product interface, authoritative documentation, or relevant subject-matter expertise: domain knowledge, accessibility criteria, or target-locale familiarity. Phase 6 Experiments 02–05 each identified at least one dimension in this context. Accuracy is a common example: a brief may establish terminology, but verifying whether a claim is factually correct requires the product itself.

**Collection-dependent** — Dimensions that require reviewing content in relation to other pieces: voice consistency across a content set, terminology uniformity across a product area, or format coherence across a family of similar articles. Phase 6 Experiment 01 established this as a distinct oversight requirement — one that is not visible when reviewing any single piece in isolation.

## When to use Phase 7

| Situation | Recommended document |
|---|---|
| You need a complete picture of what quality criteria apply to a content piece, including criteria the brief does not establish | `quality-dimensions.md` |
