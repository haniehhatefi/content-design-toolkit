## Purpose of this directory

This directory contains a detailed operational guide for each of the six stages in the AI Content Workflow. Each document provides the information needed to carry out the stage — what to prepare, what AI-assisted activities are appropriate, what the practitioner is responsible for, and what to verify before moving on.

The stage documents do not replace `master-workflow.md`. They extend it. The master workflow explains how the stages connect and when to transition between them; the stage documents explain how to work within each one.

## How to use stage documents

**Working through a task from the beginning**

If you are starting a new content task, begin with `master-workflow.md` to understand the full lifecycle and confirm the approach. Then open the stage document for stage 01 and work from there.

**Picking up work at a specific stage**

If you are entering the workflow at a particular stage — for example, beginning a content creation task with an established brief — open the relevant stage document directly. Check the entry conditions to confirm that the required inputs are in place before starting.

**Returning to an earlier stage**

If review findings or new requirements send work back to an earlier stage, open that stage document to confirm what is needed and re-establish the stage exit conditions before proceeding again.

**Working on a specific activity**

If you need guidance on a particular activity within a stage — for example, how to use AI assistance effectively during drafting, or what to check before approving a locale version — refer to the relevant stage document's AI-assisted activities and human responsibilities sections.

## Structure of each stage document

Each stage document follows a consistent ten-section structure to make the guidance easy to navigate and apply consistently across different tasks.

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| **1. Purpose and scope** | What the stage achieves and its role in the content lifecycle |
| **2. When to use AI** | When AI assistance is appropriate within this stage, and where it is not |
| **3. Required human inputs** | Information, direction, context, and materials humans must provide before AI-assisted work begins |
| **4. AI-assisted activities** | Activities AI can support at this stage |
| **5. Human responsibilities** | Human decisions, judgement, evaluation, and oversight throughout the stage |
| **6. Inputs and expected outputs** | What the stage receives and what it should produce |
| **7. Checkpoints and decision criteria** | Applicable human checkpoints, acceptance conditions, and decisions |
| **8. Stage checklist** | Practical checklist for confirming the stage's requirements are met |
| **9. Handoff to next stage** | What must be communicated or transferred before work proceeds |
| **10. Phase 4 references** | Relevant AI Content Creation principles, prompts, evaluation guidance, and supporting resources |

Not every section carries the same weight for every stage. Some stages involve substantial AI-assisted activity; others are primarily human-led. The document structure is consistent, but the depth of each section reflects the nature of the stage.

## Relationship with the master workflow

The stage documents and the master workflow serve different purposes and are designed to be used together.

| `master-workflow.md` | Stage documents |
|---|---|
| Explains how the six stages connect | Explains how to work within each stage |
| Describes the purpose and outputs of each stage at a summary level | Provides detailed activities, checklists, and guidance |
| Covers individual and team operating modes | Gives stage-specific guidance for both |
| Defines stage transitions and when work returns to an earlier stage | Confirms entry and exit conditions for each stage |
| References the five human oversight checkpoints | Explains how to apply the relevant checkpoints at each stage |

If the master workflow and a stage document appear to give different guidance on the same point, `master-workflow.md` takes precedence. Stage documents provide detail; they do not override the lifecycle structure.
