# Content Design Toolkit

A structured resource for content designers and UX writers working with AI tools. The toolkit covers voice and content principles, UX writing standards, AI-assisted content creation, an AI content workflow, practical experiments, and content quality evaluation.

---

## About this toolkit

This toolkit provides reference documentation, evaluation frameworks, and templates for content design work. It is organised as a progressive set of modules: each builds on the foundations established in earlier ones, while remaining usable as a standalone reference for the specific work it covers.

The toolkit is product-agnostic. The methods, standards, and frameworks here are designed to apply to digital content work regardless of product category, team size, or platform.

---

## Who this toolkit is for

- **Content designers and UX writers** who want structured standards and frameworks for their content work
- **Practitioners integrating AI tools** into content creation, review, or quality assessment
- **Teams establishing or reviewing content standards** across a product or content area

No prior familiarity with the toolkit is assumed. Each module's README describes its prerequisites and explains how it relates to other modules.

## Where to start

**If you are establishing or reviewing content standards,** begin with [`01-foundations/`](01-foundations/README.md). The voice principles, tone spectrum, and content principles in that module underpin the rest of the toolkit.

**If you are writing or reviewing interface copy,** begin with [`02-ux-writing-standards/`](02-ux-writing-standards/README.md). That module provides practical guidance for microcopy patterns, reusable interface components, readability, and inclusive language.

**If you are integrating AI tools into content work,** begin with [`03-ai-content-creation/`](03-ai-content-creation/README.md). That module's README describes the five-stage responsible-use workflow and explains how to navigate its documents.

**If you are applying an AI-assisted workflow to a live task,** begin with [`04-ai-content-workflow/`](04-ai-content-workflow/README.md) and open `master-workflow.md` from there.

**If you are evaluating existing content,** begin with [`06-content-quality-evaluation/`](06-content-quality-evaluation/README.md). The evaluation methodology applies whether content is in an active workflow or already published.

**If you want to see the methods applied end to end,** begin with [`05-practical-experiments/`](05-practical-experiments/README.md). The five experiments apply the toolkit's methods to constructed content scenarios and document findings at each stage, including where human judgement is required.

## How the modules connect

The modules form a progression, but each is designed to function as a standalone reference.

**`01-foundations` and `02-ux-writing-standards`** establish the voice and writing standards that the rest of the toolkit applies. Voice principles define the standard; UX writing standards explain how to apply it to specific interface patterns.

**`03-ai-content-creation` and `04-ai-content-workflow`** build on the foundation modules to provide a complete framework for AI-assisted content work. The creation module covers individual tasks — writing a prompt, evaluating an output. The workflow module covers the full lifecycle from research to delivery, with five human oversight checkpoints.

**`05-practical-experiments`** applies methods from earlier modules to realistic scenarios. Each experiment records what the method identifies and where human judgement is required.

**`06-content-quality-evaluation`** provides structured evaluation frameworks that work alongside the workflow module's checkpoints and as standalone tools for reviewing existing content.

## Conventions used throughout this toolkit

**Guidance levels** — Documents in `02-ux-writing-standards/` label each piece of guidance as Rule (must be followed), Recommendation (should be followed in most cases), or Contextual (applies with judgement). These labels are marked inline throughout those documents.

**Illustrative outputs** — Documents in `05-practical-experiments/` use outputs constructed to demonstrate specific aspects of the toolkit's methods, rather than outputs captured from actual model runs. These are labelled explicitly throughout: *Illustrative output — constructed to demonstrate [specific aspect].*
